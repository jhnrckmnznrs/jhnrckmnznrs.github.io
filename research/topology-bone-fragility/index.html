Research interface · Doctoral work

Can the shape of bone tell us something about its strength?

Trabecular bone is not simply “more bone” or “less bone.” It is a three-dimensional network whose connectivity, tunnels, cavities, orientation, and local geometry all contribute to its architecture.

Explore the idea See the research pipeline

Central question

Can topological descriptors capture aspects of trabecular architecture that complement conventional morphometry when modelling apparent bone strength?

micro-CT → structure → topology → strength

Start with the idea

What topology adds

Conventional morphometry measures quantities such as bone volume fraction, trabecular thickness, separation, connectivity density, and anisotropy. Topology asks a different question: what kinds of connected structures, loops, and enclosed voids exist, and how persistent are they as the image is filtered?

Choose your depth

One project, three ways in

The same research can be approached through intuition, mathematical structure, or implementation. The buttons below change the level of detail without changing the underlying scientific question.

Intuition

A single threshold can hide structure.

A segmented image gives one binary answer: bone or not bone. Persistent homology instead studies a sequence of related shapes. Features that appear only briefly may reflect small-scale detail or noise; features that persist across a wider part of the filtration represent structure that survives a larger change in threshold.

The goal is not to replace morphometry. It is to ask whether a different mathematical summary carries complementary information about architecture.

Mathematics

A nested family of cubical complexes.

Let f assign a scalar value to each cell of a cubical complex. For a threshold t, a sublevel filtration contains all cells whose filtration value is at most t:

Kt = { Q : f(Q) ≤ t } .

Whenever s is at most t, inclusion from Ks into Kt induces a map on homology:

Hk (Ks) → Hk (Kt) .

Persistent homology records when homology classes are born and when they die. A persistence diagram summarizes a class by a point (birth, death); its distance from the diagonal is related to how long the feature persists.

Implementation

Images become scalar fields, then topological summaries.

The topological workflow is kept separate from the conventional morphometry executable. Three-dimensional image volumes are restricted to an analysis region, converted into scalar filtrations such as image intensity or signed-distance information, and processed as cubical complexes. Persistent homology is then vectorized for statistical modelling.

This separation is intentional: the open-source bone-morphometry Rust program handles conventional and advanced non-persistent descriptors, while the persistent-homology workflow remains independently inspectable.

Research provenance

From image to model

A research interface should make the chain from data to conclusion visible. The current workflow is organized into explicit stages so that preprocessing, descriptors, and modelling can be inspected separately.

  1. 01

    micro-CT volume and analysis region

    Begin with a calibrated three-dimensional image and a defined region of interest.

  2. 02

    Preprocessing and segmentation

    Construct a binary bone representation while keeping the analysis domain explicit.

  3. 03

    Scalar filtration

    Use grayscale or distance-based scalar information to create a nested sequence of cubical complexes.

  4. 04

    Persistent homology

    Track topological classes in dimensions 0, 1, and 2 across the filtration.

  5. 05

    Vector representation

    Convert persistence information into representations suitable for comparison and statistical learning.

  6. 06

    Validation and structure–strength modelling

    Compare topological and morphometric feature families using validation procedures appropriate for a small cohort.

What comes next

Questions beyond a single descriptor

The project naturally leads to broader questions about directionality, invariance, local structure, mechanics, and learned field representations.

Direction

How should topology be adapted when trabecular architecture is strongly anisotropic?

Invariance

Which descriptors remain meaningful when the coordinate frame changes?

Mechanics

How do topological changes relate to load paths, stiffness, and localized structural weakness?

Operator learning

Can topology help neural operators preserve structural information when predicting physical fields?

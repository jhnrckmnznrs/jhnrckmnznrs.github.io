Research

From shape to measurable structure.

My work sits between mathematics, computation, and scientific data. I am particularly interested in descriptors that retain meaningful structure while remaining interpretable enough to connect mathematical features with physical questions.

Current doctoral research

Topology and bone fragility

Trabecular bone is a three-dimensional network whose mechanical behaviour depends on more than local thickness or volume fraction. I study whether topological summaries can complement conventional morphometry when describing this architecture.

Persistent homology of micro-CT images

Cubical persistent homology is used to track connected components, tunnels, and cavities across filtrations derived from image intensity and signed-distance information. Vector representations such as persistence images make these summaries usable in statistical models.

Structure–strength modelling

Topological descriptors are compared with standard bone morphometry in small-sample regression settings. The emphasis is on careful validation, interpretable feature families, and avoiding claims that exceed what the cohort can support.

Directional and frame-invariant descriptors

A related line of work develops local and directional descriptors for anisotropic bone architecture, including orientation fields and topological measurements designed to remain meaningful under changes of coordinate frame.

Scientific computing

Computational morphology at scale

Large volumetric images make seemingly simple descriptors expensive. I work on implementations that make morphology and topology practical on high-resolution three-dimensional data.

Topological data analysis

Ball Mapper and range queries

Ball Mapper is attractive because it gives a compact graph-based view of data. My work examines how its cover construction can be accelerated while keeping the mathematical meaning of the algorithm explicit.

Exact and approximate neighbourhood search

The implementation separates the Ball Mapper construction from the range-query oracle, allowing exact and approximate search backends to be studied systematically with attention to boundary conventions, invariance, and empirical performance.

Exploratory direction

Topology-aware scientific machine learning

I am interested in models that learn mappings between fields while respecting scientifically meaningful structure, including neural operators and physics-informed representations.

Neural operators

A central question is how topological summaries or losses can complement operator-learning architectures when the desired output is a field rather than a single scalar prediction.

Earlier applied machine learning

Before the doctoral project, I worked on computer-vision methods for plastic pollution monitoring and on topology-informed classification of electrocardiogram data. These projects continue to shape my preference for methods that connect computation with a concrete scientific question.

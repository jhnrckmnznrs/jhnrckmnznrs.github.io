Research tool · Prototype

Inspect trabecular bone in the browser.

This prototype is designed for fast first visualisation. It opens a small overview surface first, then replaces it with finer geometry only when more detail is useful.

The viewer starts with an overview mesh, can refine automatically through medium detail, and loads the native surface only when you explicitly request it.

How the prototype works

One segmentation, several surfaces.

The preparation step converts a binary volume into several gzip compressed GLB meshes. Coarse levels are for navigation. The final level is extracted from the native segmentation.

  1. 01

    Prepare once

    Convert an OME TIFF or TIFF segmentation into a viewer folder with a manifest and surface levels.

  2. 02

    Open the overview

    The smallest surface appears first so rotation, zoom, and pan become available quickly.

  3. 03

    Refine only when useful

    Preview and medium surfaces replace the overview without resetting the camera.

  4. 04

    Request native detail

    The largest mesh is never required simply to begin looking at the specimen.

Scientific caution.

The overview, preview, and medium meshes are visual navigation approximations. In the current converter they use max pooling before surface extraction, which can alter thin structures and topology. Use the native detail level for structural inspection when those differences matter.

Next engineering step

Replace whole specimen levels with spatial chunks.

This prototype tests the interaction model and gives us real measurements for file size, decompression, parsing, first render time, and frame rate. If the native surface becomes too large, the next version will divide it spatially and refine only the visible regions.
